Modular Flexible PCB for Self-Reconfigurable Soft Robotics

Research Lab · Rowan University · 2024

Designed modular flexible PCBs enabling self-reconfiguring soft robotic modules to communicate and share power. The project required creating a PCB that integrates motor drivers, power regulation, and communication buses onto a compact flex substrate — all while meeting the unique mechanical constraints of soft robotics.

Problem

Requirements & Constraints

Self-reconfiguring soft robots require electronics that can flex with the body, connect between modules reliably, and support motor actuation alongside communication — all in a highly constrained form factor.

Key Constraints

  • Support ESP32 wireless connectivity in each module
  • Drive two DC motors per module (e.g., DRV8837 or equivalent)
  • Integrate power regulation (buck-boost for stable 3.3V from varying battery voltage)
  • Enable module-to-module I2C communication and power sharing
  • Fit within the compact form factor of each robotic module
  • Use a flexible PCB substrate that survives repeated bending
  • ENIG finish for reliable solder joints on flex
Design

Two Design Iterations

Revision A — 2-Layer Flex

The first iteration used a 2-layer flex stackup on a single elongated PCB panel. It integrated motor drivers, power regulation, and ESP32-C3 connectivity onto one substrate. This revision validated the electrical design and communication protocols but revealed issues with component density and trace routing at the flex zones.

Revision B — 4-Layer Flex

The second revision moved to a 4-layer stackup, separating power planes from signal routing. This allowed tighter component placement, better signal integrity for the I2C bus, and more robust power distribution. The modular connector interface was refined to support reliable module-to-module docking.

Key Design Decisions

  • ENIG surface finish for reliable solder joints and corrosion resistance on flex
  • ESP32-C3-DevKitC footprint for wireless capability in each module
  • TPS63001 buck-boost converter for stable 3.3V from varying battery voltage
  • DRV8837 H-bridge motor drivers for bidirectional DC motor control
  • Castellated edges explored for module-to-module connectivity
Results & Future

Validation & Next Steps

Both revisions were fabricated and assembled in-house, with Rev B achieving reliable module-to-module communication and motor actuation. The assembled modules were validated on the physical soft robotic platform with functional connectivity between adjacent units.

Future work includes refining the mechanical docking interface for more robust self-reconfiguration, exploring rigid-flex hybrid designs to improve reliability at connector interfaces, and testing the system with larger module arrays to validate scalability of the I2C bus architecture.
