Challenge

Precision Measurement from Raw Material

The project brief was to design and manufacture a functional digital scale entirely from scratch — no off-the-shelf load cells allowed. This meant designing a custom strain-gauge-based load cell, machining it from billet aluminum, building the signal conditioning electronics, creating enclosures, and writing firmware to turn raw analog signals into calibrated weight readings.

Key Requirements

  • Custom-machined load cell with predictable deflection characteristics
  • FEA validation before committing to CNC machining
  • Multi-unit display (grams, ounces, pounds)
  • Tare function for container compensation
  • IP31 enclosure to protect electronics from light dust and dripping
  • All manufacturing done in-house using university shop equipment
Design & Simulation

FEA-Validated Load Cell Design

The load cell was designed in Fusion 360 as a cantilever beam geometry machined from 6010 aluminum alloy. The beam cross-section was optimized to produce measurable deflection under the target load range (0–3 kg) while staying well within the elastic region of the material.

Before machining, the design was validated using SolidWorks Simulation (static stress analysis). The FEA confirmed that peak Von Mises stress under maximum load remained below the yield strength with an adequate safety factor, and that the predicted deflection at the strain gauge mounting location would produce sufficient signal amplitude for the HX711 ADC to resolve.

Manufacturing Process

  • CNC milled the load cell beam from 6010 aluminum billet on a 3-axis Haas
  • Manual lathe operations for precision cylindrical features
  • Surface finishing to ensure clean strain gauge bonding
  • Strain gauges bonded and wired in a Wheatstone bridge configuration
  • ABS enclosure 3D printed in a clamshell design with heat-set threaded inserts
Electronics & Firmware

Signal Conditioning & Calibration

The electronics stack uses an HX711 24-bit ADC to read the Wheatstone bridge output from the strain gauges, connected to an Arduino microcontroller. The firmware implements a multi-point calibration routine that maps raw ADC counts to calibrated weight values, with temperature compensation for thermal drift.

User-facing features include real-time LCD weight display, unit switching between grams/ounces/pounds, a tare function for zeroing out container weight, and an auto-power-off timer for battery conservation. The clamshell ABS enclosure was designed with recessed button wells and a display window, achieving an IP31 ingress protection rating to guard against light dust and vertical dripping.
