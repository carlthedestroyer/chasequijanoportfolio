Overview

A Sensing Stack Looking for a Navigation Loop

I built a mobile robot platform running ROS 2 Iron on a Linux-based mini PC, with a Teensy microcontroller driving the motors through a stepper driver breakout board. The sensing side is real and working: an RPLiDAR A1 and an Intel RealSense depth camera both publish cleanly into ROS 2, giving the robot 2D scan data and a full 3D point cloud of its surroundings. What's not there yet is the navigation loop that would turn that sensor data into autonomous driving.

A 22.2V 6000mAh LiPo battery powers the system, with a step-down voltage regulator managing power for the primary electronics and computer. The robot also features a custom cooling solution with 10mm fans to handle thermal management for the onboard mini PC.

Navigation & Mapping

RPLiDAR and RealSense Point Cloud Mapping

The RPLiDAR A1 provides a 360-degree planar scan of the environment, used for localization and obstacle detection. The live capture below shows the LiDAR sweeping a room, with the white line marking the current scan angle and red points marking detected surfaces.

The Intel RealSense depth camera complements the 2D LiDAR scan with a full RGB point cloud, giving the robot a 3D understanding of the space it is navigating, including furniture and other obstacles outside the LiDAR's scan plane.

Reflection & Current Status

Where This Actually Stands

Getting the RPLiDAR and RealSense streams both publishing cleanly into ROS 2 was the biggest hurdle, and that part is real and working - the scan and point cloud captures on this page are live sensor data, not simulated. What I didn't get to before setting this project aside is the navigation loop: SLAM, an occupancy map fused from both sensors, and closed-loop autonomous driving on top of that data. The robot senses its environment; it doesn't yet act on what it senses.

Picking this back up would mean building the navigation stack on top of an already-working sensor pipeline, not starting from bring-up.
