Leadership

Revitalizing a Dormant Chapter

When I took over as President in September 2025, Rowan's SAME chapter had gone dormant - no active officer board, no faculty advisor relationship, no running projects. The chapter's mission is to educate and inspire engineering students interested in the defense and aerospace industry, through both technical projects and hands-on STEM outreach - none of that happens without people and a working relationship with the department.

I recruited a cross-disciplinary student officer board and rebuilt the chapter's connections with its faculty executives, restarting regular meetings and re-establishing SAME Rowan as an active student chapter rather than a line on a club roster. That foundation is what made it possible to go after outside funding a few months later.

Grant Proposal

Authoring the $4,640 Winning Proposal

I authored the technical proposal to the SAME Philadelphia Post - an 8-section document covering the problem statement, technical solution, specifications, testing plan, bill of materials, milestones, and risk mitigation - co-written with fellow members Jakub Gajda and Brian Smith, and directed the electrical and power-distribution subframe architecture for the aircraft.

The pitch: municipalities and emergency responders are stuck choosing between $10,000+ professional search-and-rescue drones or consumer drones with limited payload and range. We proposed a low-cost, 2-hour-endurance, 3.5kg-payload fixed-wing UAV that closes that gap - a woven carbon-fiber airframe with a V-tail empennage, designed to deliver medical supplies to disaster areas on a single charge.

Technical Solution

Assembly Architecture & Fabrication Plan

  • Fuselage: woven carbon-fiber unibody, cast as one piece to house avionics, power, and the motor while cutting weight and adding stiffness; outfitted with interchangeable modular sensor packages
  • Empennage: a carbon-fiber rod boom in place of a standard bulky tail structure, carrying a V-tail with symmetrical-airfoil ruddervators that combine elevator and rudder into one control surface - fewer actuators, simpler build
  • Wing: two-piece wing using a NACA 2412 airfoil, chosen for strong low-speed lift suited to slow, long-endurance, and loitering search patterns

Fabrication follows a low-cost-first sequence: FDM, SLA, and laser cutting for rapid, cheap prototyping and design validation, then a final woven carbon-fiber composite build for the production airframe - chosen for its strength-to-weight ratio and resistance to the weather and salt-air corrosion search operations actually get performed in.

Testing & Validation Plan

From FEA to Flight Trials

The proposal lays out a full validation campaign spanning digital and physical testing before the aircraft flies a real mission:

  • Simulation & FEA: Simulink and CAD-based stability simulation for payload release and severe weather; stress/strain FEA on the airframe to confirm a 1.5 Factor of Safety
  • Physical testing: power-system thermal testing across the full 2-hour flight envelope, center-of-gravity testing with the payload loaded, and thrust/efficiency testing to tune motor speed for endurance
  • Flight trials: low-altitude stability testing first, a dummy-payload release test to check for stability and drop-damage, and a full 2-hour endurance test at the 3.5 kg payload target

Prototyping

Low-Cost Technology Demonstrator

Ahead of committing to a full carbon-fiber layup, the club built a hand-assembled balsa-and-foam flying-wing demonstrator - laser-cut balsa wing ribs, a foam-core boom, and an off-the-shelf motor/ESC - to validate the aerodynamic layout and get hands-on build experience before working in composite. It's the same low-cost-prototyping philosophy from the proposal's fabrication plan, just applied at club scale.

Outreach

STEM Education & Community Outreach

Technical projects are half the chapter's mission - the other half is outreach. SAME Rowan attends workshops and runs its own table at community STEM days, introducing K-12 students to engineering and the defense and aerospace field through hands-on activities. At the SAME Philadelphia Post's STEM Day, we exhibited as the Rowan University Student Chapter, and later staffed our own outreach tent with build demos, flyers, and a printout of the reconnaissance drone project for kids to see real hardware up close.
